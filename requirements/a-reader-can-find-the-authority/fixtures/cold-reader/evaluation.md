# Cold-reader evaluation: find the authority

This is advisory validation. It is not an acceptance test and no wall calls a
model or grades comprehension.

## Ground

Give a reader a clean checkout at the candidate revision. Give them no commit
history, external links, search results, or explanation of KAAL's vocabulary.
They begin at `README.md` and may follow the repository links it exposes.

## Prompt

Answer from the repository route and cite the local authority for each answer:

1. What is KAAL?
2. What problem does it solve?
3. Who is it for?
4. What can be installed and used today?
5. What is current capability, committed next work, and longer-term projection?
6. How does one ask become a released change?
7. Which file answers each question in that journey?
8. Which artefact is authoritative when nearby files discuss the same subject?
9. Who owns each artefact and where does it belong?
10. Which artefacts are wants, decisions, evidence, derived views, or explanation?

## What the evaluator judges

- The answer establishes the practical job before explaining seats, lanes,
  walls, pins, or records.
- It describes KAAL as software delivery, engine oriented, and does not call it
  a product, runtime, or repository operating system.
- It separates what is installable now, what a current plan commits next, and
  what the design projects beyond that.
- Every material claim cites a local authority reached from the entrance.
- It treats seat backlogs as authored block records and `kaal backlog` as the
  derived reading of them, not as their generator.
- When an explanation and an authority discuss the same fact, it identifies
  which one owns the fact.
- It can name an artefact's owner, home, and information kind without inferring
  any of them from commit history.

## Record

Record the candidate revision, reader, date, answers, and the evaluator's
judgement. A disagreement is evidence for another requirement or a correction
to this one. It does not turn a deterministic wall green or red.
