# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the one-hundred-sixth use of the analyse skill, a second cycle on
`a-block-follows-a-declared-edge` after the asker answered its three open
questions and asked for a stronger proof, 14 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The open questions were worth asking, and two of the three answers changed
  the page rather than confirming it. The ninth edge is not missing, it is
  transitive; and the three edges no kind can express became a criterion
  instead of a note. A page that had assumed either way would have been
  wrong, and the one that asked was not.
- The asker's instruction to exercise every pair found nothing and proved
  something. Seven mutations, and the one that hard coded the single sampled
  rejection reds criterion 3 exactly as intended. A test that samples cannot
  tell a wall from a lookup table, and now this one can.
- Stating a capability without prescribing its mechanism worked out cleanly.
  Criterion 9 says every declared edge must be writable as a block and names
  no kind; the command reports which seat owes which kind, so the criterion is
  checkable and the vocabulary stays the builder's.

## Learned

- A fixture that lists what the tree declares will drift from it. The
  fixtures named the four kinds, so once criterion 9 existed every fixture
  tree declared eight edges over a vocabulary too small for three of them, and
  answered with criterion 9's finding while testing something else. Reading
  `blocks` from the tree's own declaration fixed it and had a second effect
  worth more: the test file now names no kind at all, so it cannot prescribe
  one by accident.
- Two of the three answers were reachable from the tree and one was not. That
  architecture reaches the developer transitively is visible in the edges as
  soon as somebody says transitive reach is not an edge; that the analyst's
  empty page is correct follows from a person not being a seat. Neither was
  derivable without the asker saying which reading was theirs, and guessing
  would have produced a page that looked settled and was not.
- Running the board is a different assertion from reading the config. The
  first version of criterion 8 checked that `kaal.config.json` named a gate,
  which is true of a gate that never runs, never fails, or fails always. The
  mutation that refuses every block passes that check and reds the real one.

## Lacked

- Nothing in the skill about a second cycle on a task already stated. The
  handoff is written as though a requirement is stated once; this page has now
  been through an asker's answers and a strengthening pass, and nothing says
  whether that is an amendment, a supersede or the same statement getting
  better. It is the third, and the page says so only because I wrote it.
- No rule about a criterion that samples. "One criterion, one test" and
  "count first" both aim at this and neither reaches it: a test that asserts
  one of thirty six cases satisfies both and proves almost nothing. The
  asker's instruction is the rule the skill is missing.

## Longed for

- A way to say a criterion is a capability rather than a mechanism. Criterion
  9 had to carry its own sentence explaining that it names no kind on purpose,
  because nothing in the shape of a criterion distinguishes "this must be
  possible" from "this must be done this way".
