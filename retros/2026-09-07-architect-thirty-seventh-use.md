# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the thirty-seventh use of the architect skill, on
`architecture/a-decision-balances-two-goods` (three seams, on the skill's
own decision record), 7 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- Writing the records under the shape the task adds. The four decisions
  here carry a `Bought:` line the template does not have yet, which made
  the change readable before it existed and forced me to price four
  choices while arguing that pricing is worth doing. Two of the four cost
  something I would not have written down otherwise.
- Seam 3 came from asking what the change forecloses, which is the rule
  being added. A template that grows a field could redden thirty-eight
  drawings written before it grew. It does not, and now a test says so on
  a root of its own rather than on the league's.

## Learned

- A contract on a template must read the section it is about. Test 2
  compared the labels of the whole page and went red on `Fixed:`, `Free:`,
  `Task:` and four handoff lines, none of which is the decision shape. It
  was red before the build and would have stayed red after it, which is the
  worst kind of red: the colour is right and the reason is not.
- A green contract before the build can be the point rather than a defect.
  Seam 3 says the wall's verdict on a four-line drawing does not change. It
  is true now and has to stay true after the template grows, so a test that
  only went green at the end would have been testing something else.
- Pricing a decision changes what you write in it. The record on not
  retrofitting the old drawings would have read as obviously free. Naming
  what it spends produced the honest sentence: a reader of an old record
  cannot tell whether the choice was free or merely unpriced, and nothing
  marks which.

## Lacked

- Nothing says what to do with a seam that serves no criterion. Seam 3
  holds a constraint rather than an observable the asker named, and the
  strategy table has a column for criteria and no row for a promise that
  belongs to nobody's criterion. I wrote a paragraph under the table, which
  works and is not the template's shape.
- The template still has nowhere to record a fact established by running
  something, which is now the fourth task in a row to hit it, and this time
  I checked the wall's source to learn that it does not read decision
  fields at all. That fact is load bearing for seam 3 and it lives in a
  sentence a reader has to trust.

## Longed for

- A worked example in the skill of a decision priced badly. The one this
  tree has is mine, in a closed drawing, and closed drawings are not
  retrofitted, so the example cannot be pointed at from the rule.
