# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the sixty-fourth use of the code skill, building the block: a widened
sub key, a kind read from a block as well as a line, and `writePins` writing
into one, 11 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The fold is where the second writing stops. A block becomes the value shape
  inside `readTrace` and every reader below that point sees what it always
  saw, so one grammar gained a writing and no wall learned a second one.
- The widening is one character class and the comment beside it now carries
  both reasons, the slash that came with `reviews:` and the dot that comes
  with a case path. A reader meeting the pattern in a year sees why it is
  shaped that way rather than a regular expression somebody widened twice.

## Learned

- Ten contracts and six of them red was the whole specification. Nothing here
  needed a decision, because the drawing had made them all, including the two
  the stand-in found: that a block says none by holding no entry and that the
  pin belongs to the seam that writes rather than the seam that resolves.
- `plans.mjs` reads the block itself rather than through `readTrace`, because
  it answers about pages before the trace wall has read them. That is a second
  reader of the block, which is the thing the sixty-third use was a correction
  for, and it is justified only by the order the two run in. It is the first
  place to look when the block form next behaves differently in two walls.

## Lacked

- No rule about a reader that must run before the parser everybody else uses.
  `plans.mjs` has one because it reads a page to decide whether it is worth
  reading, and the league says one grammar, one reader, with no exception
  written for the case where the reader is what decides.

## Longed for

- A `readTrace` cheap enough that nothing needs to read a page ahead of it.
  Then the exception above disappears rather than being justified.
