# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the sixty-sixth use of the architect skill, moving seam 7 of
`a-suite-names-its-cases` from a seat's tree to anything the board says owns a
path, 11 September 2026. One seam, two halves, one contract.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The seam grew rather than split. What owns a case is one promise and it now
  has two halves, `owners(root)` gathering what the board declares and
  `caseOwner(path, owners)` answering about one path, which keeps the question
  in one place and lets the contract drive both ends of it.
- Nothing else moved. Nine of the ten contracts were green before this diff
  and are green after, which is what a seam list is for.

## Learned

- A seam that was green as a guard can go red again, and that is not a
  regression. Seam 7 was named in the handoff as a promise kept by the diff
  before, and then the criterion it serves widened, so the promise grew and
  has to be kept a second time. The handoff now says which of the two it is
  rather than carrying yesterday's reason.
- The widening cost one word in the finding and it is the word that matters.
  `no seat owns it` became `nothing owns it`, because a lane owns too, and a
  finding that names the wrong kind of owner sends a reader to the wrong page
  of the config.

## Lacked

- No way for a drawing to say a seam's promise grew. The list reads as though
  seam 7 had always said `owners`, and only the handoff's red run line, which
  is about this diff rather than about the seam, records that it once said
  seats.

## Longed for

- A seam that carries the criterion it serves, so that a criterion widening
  shows up as the seam going red rather than as somebody noticing.

Tensions weighed: `the-two-goods`, once, and it was not close. Reading both
declarations costs one more line and buys the league's own skills being
coverable by the league's own method.
