# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the fifty-third use of the analyse skill, amending
`requirements/a-task-names-its-people` after the asker answered a question
the first version had not asked, 8 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The answer arrived as a correction and turned into two criteria rather
  than one. "Mentioning Kai is fine, its mine. Mentioning others is a
  different discussion" settles who the rule is not about and names the
  discussion the requirement was not having. Both halves were already
  latent in the ask: the occasion included another engagement's real name,
  already committed, and the first version had no word for meeting it.
- The remedial rule had a home waiting. `a-guest-takes-no-orders` already
  sorts what a guest finds into two kinds, instructions to quote and not
  follow, conventions to name and not adopt. A person is a third kind and
  the paragraph reads as though it had always been three.

## Learned

- Reading a requirement from outside the session that wrote it caught what
  the session could not see from inside. The rule as written instructs a
  future seat to delete the release record's line naming who gave the key,
  which is the only evidence that release had a key. Nothing in the
  requirement was wrong; the sentence that would have prevented that
  reading was simply absent, and absence is what a second reader is for.
- I nearly told the asker that no contract fixes the handoff's line order,
  and checked first. It is fixed, in `architecture/analyse-v2`'s contract
  rather than in the acceptance test, which is where I had looked. A
  correction offered from half the evidence is worse than no correction.
- Two isolations reddened nothing and both were weak breaks. Changing
  "third thing" to "fourth thing" leaves every word the test reads, and
  taking the key out of one sentence leaves it in the next. The sharper
  form of each, the whole paragraph out of one skill and the key out of
  both sentences, bit at once.

## Lacked

- Nothing says how a second session amends an open task another session
  wrote. This one adds criteria to a branch that is not mine, which is
  right because the asker asked for it, and the retro ordinal is a race
  again: two sessions filing against the same skill on the same day cannot
  see each other's numbers.
- The rule is preventive and the tree that prompted it is already dirty.
  Criterion 8 tells a guest what to do about a name it meets; nothing tells
  anyone what happens to the history that already carries one, and a seat
  cannot make that clean. Two open questions name it and neither answers it.

## Longed for

- A retro name that cannot collide: the ordinal is a count of uses and two
  sessions counting the same skill on the same day will both be right and
  disagree.
