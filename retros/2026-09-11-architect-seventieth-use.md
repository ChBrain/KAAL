# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the seventieth use of the architect skill, drawing the seam for the
criterion this drawing refused to write, 11 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The loop closed the way it is supposed to. This drawing named a gap and
  built nothing for it, the analyst made it a criterion the same afternoon,
  and the seam is drawn against a criterion somebody else wrote. Refusing was
  cheaper than being right about it.
- One seam, one function, one boolean. A wall's colour is the same fact at
  both targets and the smallest thing that can differ is the exit code, so
  nothing about what a wall reports moves.

## Learned

- A criterion arriving after its drawing is merged is a deadlock with exactly
  one way out, and nothing says which way. The strategy table went one row
  short and the drawings wall answered `criteria not in the strategy table: 9`.
  The row is in `architecture/` and the criterion is in `requirements/`, which
  is two lanes, and the board refuses either half alone. The wall reads a row
  for a criterion that is not there yet and says nothing; it reads a criterion
  with no row and refuses. So the drawing goes first, and that is a fact about
  this one wall rather than a rule anybody wrote down.
- The refusal to draw an unstated criterion cost one merge conflict and one
  afternoon, and it was still right. The alternative was this drawing writing
  the analyst's page, which is the thing the seats wall exists to stop and the
  thing no wall can catch when it happens inside one head.
- `kaal traces --write` reached into the analyst's requirement again, from
  this branch, and the seats wall caught it again. Second time today, same
  command, same direction.

## Lacked

- A rule for which half of a two lane change goes first. It exists per wall,
  by accident, and a reader has to work it out from what each wall tolerates.

## Longed for

- A drawing that can say `this criterion is in review` rather than either
  drawing it or refusing it. The two halves of one change were correct for
  four hours and could not be merged for three of them.
