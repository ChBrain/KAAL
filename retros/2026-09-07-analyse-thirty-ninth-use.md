# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the thirty-ninth use of the analyse skill, on
`requirements/a-decision-balances-two-goods` (four criteria, on the
architect's decision record), 7 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The ask arrived with its own evidence an hour old. Kai named two goods in
  tension and the failure was already in the tree: the class drawing chose
  the shortest path, took no workflow change, and foreclosed the wall it
  existed to deliver. Writing an assumption that cites a defect I made
  myself this afternoon is a better argument than any example I could have
  invented.
- Isolating the tests took three partial stand-ins and each criterion fell
  on its own. Dropping one paragraph reddened one and two, dropping the
  next reddened three, dropping one template line reddened four. The full
  stand-in alone would not have shown that.

## Learned

- A criterion on prose is a criterion on an argument's shape, not on a
  word. My first version of test three matched a regex assembled from the
  sentence I imagined the developer would write, which is the same defect
  a review found in a contract test this morning: an author's memory
  standing in for a rule. It now reads the sentences that make the claim
  and asks whether the reason sits with them.
- Two goods in tension is a different rule from "a decision with no options
  was not a decision", which the skill already carries. The old rule is
  about whether a choice existed; the new one is about what it cost. A
  record can satisfy the first completely and still hide the price, which
  is what made this a task rather than a rewording.

## Lacked

- Nothing helps decide whether a rule about how a seat thinks should also
  become a wall. Three of the four criteria are text a reader must honour
  and no runner can check that a price named is a price paid. I put it in
  the open questions, which is right, and the skill offers no way to say
  "this one is a discipline on purpose".
- The requirement template still has nowhere to record a fact established
  by running something, which is now the third task in a row to hit it.
  Here it is a defect in this tree's own history rather than a third
  party's tool, and it went into an assumption again.

## Longed for

- A worked example in the skill of a decision priced badly, since the one
  in this tree is mine and closed drawings are not retrofitted.
