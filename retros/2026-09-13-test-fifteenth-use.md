# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the fifteenth use of the test skill, recording what a kept promise
proved, 13 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The wall's own count is now a true sentence about this tree. It read three
  not judged when the record was written, two once this task's record landed,
  and one once the superseded task was recorded again. Each number was right
  when it was printed and none of them needed a person to interpret it.
- A supersede paid its own bill without anybody remembering to. Changing that
  task's proof made its record stale, the coverage wall said so by name, and
  the fix was to run it and write down what came back.

## Learned

- A supersede costs two records and not one. The task doing the superseding
  needs its own, and the task superseded needs a new one because its proof
  moved, and only the second is easy to walk past: nothing about writing the
  first would have reminded anybody of the second.
- The wall that motivated this task is also the one that reports the cost. It
  counted the stale record as unjudged, which is exactly right and is the
  first time a wall in this tree has told the tester what work it owes rather
  than a person noticing.

## Lacked

- Anything that pairs a supersede with the records it invalidates. The
  coverage wall names a missing record and never says why it went missing,
  so a reader sees a task fall off the list and has to reconstruct which diff
  pushed it.

## Longed for

- A record that carries the reason it was rewritten. This one says a suite
  passed on a date and nothing about the supersede that made the last one
  stop speaking, which is the interesting half.
