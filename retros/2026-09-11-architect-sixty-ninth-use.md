# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the sixty-ninth use of the architect skill, drawing
`a-promotion-names-what-it-refuses`, 11 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- Eight seams and not one of them decides anything about a suite, a wall or a
  lane. The four verdicts are one function already, the board is one function
  already, and the lane is one function already; every seam here is a sort
  over an answer this tree gives. The stand-in was under a hundred lines
  because of it.
- The stand-in went green on all eight and was discarded from file copies.
  Two seams were red on the shape of a reader rather than on their promise,
  which is exactly what a stand-in is for: `runGates` answers `results` and
  not `walls`, and a contract written against the wrong name would have been
  handed to the developer as a fact.
- Seam 8 was red on its own account from the first run. The others shared the
  red of a module that does not exist, which is what a new module's first red
  is, but the seat rule is there and answers `release: matches no lane`.

## Learned

- The fourth copy of the same two words was what made the first seam. Two
  lists already name `release` and `main`, in `class.mjs` and in the workflow,
  and neither knows about the other. A seam that holds no logic is worth
  drawing when the thing it holds is a fact three readers disagree about.
- `kaal traces --write` writes into pages outside the caller's lane. It put a
  sha on the analyst's requirement from an architect branch and the seats wall
  caught it, which is the wall doing its job about a tool that should not have
  needed catching. A command that writes is a command with a lane.
- A drawing that answers its requirement leaves things a reader wants the same
  day. The pre-push hook enforces the strict gate on every branch, so a wall
  standing red on `release` cannot be pushed at all; and `main` can move
  without `release` by a hotfix, with nothing reading the pair. Both were
  found by running, neither is in the eight criteria, and widening the seams to
  cover them would be this drawing writing criteria.

## Lacked

- A way to say `this is the analyst's` that outlives the diff. Two gaps go
  into a decision section, and a decision section is read by whoever opens
  this drawing, which is the architect.

## Longed for

- One reader for the board that a promotion can ask rather than run. The
  decision to run it is right and it costs a board run inside a gate that is
  already running one.
