# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the sixty-fourth use of the architect skill, redrawing
`a-suite-names-its-cases` after the asker lifted the writing and the sha out
of the criteria and settled that both edges are written the same way,
11 September 2026. Seven seams became ten.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The criteria getting smaller made the drawing bigger in the right place.
  With the writing no longer stated above, the block, the widened key and
  `writePins` into a block are three promises this seat owes and tests, where
  before they were a sentence in somebody else's page.
- Four contracts were green before the build and are named in the handoff
  rather than quietly counted. Their promises did not move when the writing
  did, and each is now a guard: a suite name still resolves under
  `tests/suites/`, a case path still resolves to itself, `tests/` is still
  refused before ownership is asked.

## Learned

- The stand-in found two, and both were the block and the line disagreeing
  about how to say none. A contract was still asserting the pin from the line
  form, which belongs to the seam that writes a block and now has its own
  contract. And a fixture wrote a case literally called `nothing`, because a
  line says none with a word and a block says it by holding no entry. Two
  writings of one grammar means two ways to say nothing, and that is the first
  thing a reader of either will get wrong.
- A widened shared reader is a seam for every reader, and naming them is what
  makes the change small. The parser is read by the skills, the records, the
  waivers, the retros and the reviews block, and the seam says what all five
  keep: exactly the behaviour they have, with the only difference being which
  keys are read rather than dropped in silence.
- A drawing that grows by three seams after its build has begun is not a
  failure of the drawing; it is what a measurement bought. Nobody knew the
  list was 3,557 characters until somebody tried to write it.

## Lacked

- No word for a seam that exists to keep an old promise rather than to make a
  new one. Four of the ten here are green on the day they are written, and the
  skill's rule is that a test green before the build is named with its reason,
  which says what to write and not what such a seam is called.
- Nothing about renumbering. Inserting three seams in the middle moved five
  contracts and six rows of a table, all by hand, and the only check that any
  of it agreed afterwards is that the numbers still count the same.

## Longed for

- A check that the test strategy's rows name seams that exist. Today it would
  have caught the row still pointing at seam 4 after seam 4 became seam 7.

Tensions weighed: `the-two-goods` on both new decisions, and
`the-seat-owns-the-lens` on the widened parser, where one shared reader serves
five callers and the change belongs to none of them. No tension went unnamed.
