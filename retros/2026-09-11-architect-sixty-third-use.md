# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the sixty-third use of the architect skill, removing the counts seam
from `the-test-tree-is-written-down` because `a-suite-names-its-cases`
supersedes the two criteria it served, 11 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The decision record stayed and was reopened rather than deleted. A reader of
  the seam list will ask why a count was ever in a page, and the record is the
  only place that answers; a decision is history and history is not amended
  by removal.
- The supersede was already declared, so nothing had to be argued here. The
  requirement said which two criteria move and the drawing followed, which is
  the trace grammar doing the work a conversation would otherwise do.

## Learned

- A seam whose promise is withdrawn goes; a seam whose promise changed is
  amended. This one was withdrawn: without globs there is no number for a
  count to agree with, so neither half of seam 4 survives the supersede, and
  keeping it as a seam that promises nothing would have left a contract with
  nothing to assert.
- Two fixtures went with it. A fixture exists for a case and a case that is no
  longer asked leaves a tree nobody drives, which is a green directory that
  proves nothing. The fixtures wall lists them; it does not say which are
  still reached.

## Lacked

- Nothing in the skill about removing a seam. Everything it says is about
  drawing one: name it, test it, number the picture to match. Renumbering the
  ones after it, and what happens to the fixtures the removed contract drove,
  are both left to whoever notices.
- No way to say in the drawing that a seam is gone rather than never drawn.
  The list now runs one to four and a reader has no reason to think it ever
  ran to five, which the handoff says in one line and the seams section does
  not say at all.

## Longed for

- A fixture wall that answers which fixtures no test reaches. Two were found
  by hand today and only because the contract that drove them was the one
  being removed.

Tensions weighed: `the-two-goods`, once. Removing the seam buys the shortest
path to a tree that says what is true, and spends the reader's ability to see
that a promise used to be there, which the reopened decision is meant to pay
back. No tension went unnamed.
