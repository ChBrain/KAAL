# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the fifty-eighth use of the code skill, on
`requirements/a-seat-claims-what-it-covers` (seven criteria, five seams, the
thirteenth wall and the first that cannot fail), 9 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The drawing owed the build two supersedes and named both before I started,
  so neither was a discovery mid change. Criterion 6's test asked for a
  mechanism that does not exist and `gates-v1` fixed a board that could not
  carry this wall's answer. Knowing that on the way in turned two arguments
  into two edits.
- A wall that cannot fail is a smaller idea than it looked. The board learned
  one thing, a gate saying its line belongs on the board, and it generalises
  to any wall whose output is the answer rather than a count of tests.
- The strategy table said no unit suite and it was right. The counting sits
  behind two seams and a unit of it would have been the contract test with
  the seam taken out, which is a second copy of a test rather than a second
  reading.

## Learned

- A unit that asserts two commands never refuse in the same words held that
  claim across twelve commands and fired on the thirteenth. `runs` and
  `coverage` both read `requirements/<task>/requirement.md` and both said so
  in the same sentence, so a reader would learn which file was missing and
  not which question had died. `release` and `class` had already paid this
  and their reasons name the question first. The assertion is a year of
  patience for one afternoon of use, and that is the trade a countable
  invariant makes.
- I would not have found it by reading. The two cases sit sixteen lines
  apart and I wrote the second by copying the first, which is exactly the
  shape a distinctness check exists to catch and exactly the shape a reviewer
  skims past.
- The board's format wall disagreed with the formatter for one run. A sweep
  of the whole tree reported the file unchanged and the check still refused
  it; writing that one file by name fixed it. I do not know which of the two
  was wrong and I did not chase it, because the wall is the authority and it
  went green. Worth naming so the next person who sees it does not assume
  their edit is at fault.

## Lacked

- Nothing says where a supersede the drawing owes actually lands. The
  drawing wrote both down in its Handoff, which is the right place for the
  architect to owe it and the wrong place to find it later: the pin ended up
  in the requirement's frontmatter and the reason in its Handoff, and no
  rule said it would.
- No way for a wall to say how much of its output belongs on the board. The
  flag is a yes, so this wall's three rows are on the board in full and a
  wall with thirty rows would be too. The drawing named that as what reopens
  the decision and it will be a second wall that reopens it, not this one.

## Longed for

- A coverage record, pinned like a run record. Every number here reports and
  none of them refuses, which the requirement's own open question already
  names: a pinned record would turn a drop from an opinion into a
  regression, and the delivery report showed exactly how that reads.
- The operator's row. Three seats are on the board and the fourth has one
  release and one record to count, which is a row nobody learns from. The
  table makes it a line whenever there is something to put in it.
