# Retrospective: 4 L's

## Liked

- Existing requirements separated the already held lane rule from the new ask.

## Learned

- Only the manage skill currently names Plan, Do, Check and Act.
- Manage currently says available work is never written and Act ends at open,
  both of which the new ask moves.
- A seat's claim lifecycle and the manager's derived live view can fail and land
  independently even though the second depends on the first.
- Contract language and lifecycle behavior are separate observables. Phrase
  matching can prove the first while allowing a ready pull request with no
  claim or unfinished work.
- Normalized semantic fixtures can hold the lifecycle decision still without
  choosing the provider adapter, backlog serialization or enforcing wall.
- A negative ordering proof needs its positive inverse: otherwise a judge that
  rejects every non-empty durable order can satisfy the suite.

## Lacked

- Analyse says to take the first task after a split, but gives the second earned
  task no durable home beyond the first task's handoff.
- The current backlog can record only blocks, so the analyst could not claim
  this work using the proposed model without assuming its implementation.
- The first handoff had no behavioral seam. Its six tests could all turn green
  without any repository-owned claim affecting a pull request's readiness.
- Criterion 7 initially proved two red states but no correctly ordered green
  state, so it did not constrain the judge's acceptance behavior.

## Longed for

- A repository-owned ask record could preserve every task produced by analysis
  before the manager places any of them.
- The next analysis could resolve a named dependent task without reconstructing
  it from conversation.
- Analyse guidance could explicitly ask whether contract wording is itself the
  behavior or whether a deterministic decision surface must also be proved.
