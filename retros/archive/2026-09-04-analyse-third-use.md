# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the third real use of the analyse skill, on the ask that arrived as
a refusal: the pre-push hook would not let a push through because an open
requirement's red criterion gated the board. 4 September 2026.

## Liked

- The ask had already been named in two retros as Lacked; when it became a
  blocker the requirement wrote itself in minutes, because the loop had done
  the thinking earlier.
- Four criteria were enough, and the fourth ("the board is green while
  push-v1 is open") is the whole point stated as a test.

## Learned

- A wall that refuses its own author's push is the best evidence the wall
  works; the refusal was the first real use of the hook and it was correct.
- The requirement's status belongs in the requirement, in one line, and the
  analyst owns both ends of it: open at handoff, closed when green.

## Lacked

- A test that read a line's status regardless of where the status sat; the
  first draft assumed an order the developer was free to choose.
- A rule for a task that needs a person's step to close: push-v1 stays open
  until two eval records exist, and "open" is now honest, but the board
  cannot tell "waiting on a person" from "waiting on a developer".

## Longed for

- The retros consumed by a requirement being archived by the same act that
  closes it.
