# Retrospective: 4 L's

Self-diagnosis: drawn from session context. Not a human-reported retro.

Period: the thirty-seventh use of the analyse skill, on
`requirements/a-change-declares-its-class` (four criteria, the wall that
makes a rule of a promise), 7 September 2026.
Place: this repository

## Liked

- The ask's two halves decided the design between them. The first, that
  minor and major are Kai's, makes the class computable rather than
  declared. The second, that the step to 0.1.x is a decision, says the
  refusal is about the version and never about the surface. Without the
  second I would have written a wall that turns red on every new command.
- The requirement closes a gap the code skill has been naming for days: it
  tells the developer to give the class the tooling computes, and nothing
  computed one. The criterion is not my idea; it is an unpaid debt.

## Learned

- A command that reads history cannot be proven on a directory of files,
  so the fixtures are git repositories the tests build. The twenty-fifth
  architect retro asked for exactly that a day ago and called it a task of
  its own; it turned out to be this one, arriving by necessity rather than
  by anyone choosing it.
- Writing criterion 4 made this change move the surface, which the command
  it describes will then report. That is not a paradox, it is the tool
  working on its first run, and it is worth seeing.

## Lacked

- No guidance on a criterion whose subject is a diff. Everything in the
  skill assumes a surface a test can drive; here the subject is the
  difference between two states, and building the second state was most of
  the test.
- Nothing says what an analyst does with a question that cannot be
  answered yet. Three of the four open questions here are only answerable
  after the surface settles, and the skill treats an open question as a
  thing to be resolved rather than a thing to be waited out.

## Longed for

- A way to mark an open question as one that time answers rather than a
  person, so a later reader does not mistake patience for neglect.
